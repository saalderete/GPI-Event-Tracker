## Why this project earns the right to exist

**The problem is real for a defined group.** In Phase 2, six of twelve people described missing events they would have attended on a recurring basis, and seven of twelve, asked an open question about what they would change, asked for a single place to see what is coming. The people it fails hardest are the ones who rely on word of mouth and the ones with little or no social media. See the [Market Research](../market-research/) for the counts.

**The incumbents are not built for this job.** Instagram shows people what they already follow. Google returns places, not events. Ticketmaster covers big shows. FitFam is one account's curation, known to the people who know it. Each is good at its own job and none of them is a listing of what is happening in El Paso this weekend.

**Demand for the specific shape has been demonstrated once already.** One participant described a page that "would put all of the events that were going on every week," and that they used until it stopped. Another asked for "a weekly event round-up or calendar." The ask is not novel; it is unmet.

**It is buildable by this team in a semester.** The core is still a listing with dates and categories. Personalization and paid listings are in the charter's scope, but they come after a reliable supply of events and a useful listing. The hard part is sourcing accurate event information across El Paso and Juárez, and that is a process and partnership problem the team can plan, not a technology the team cannot obtain (which is what stopped Candidate 5).

## Who it serves first

1. **People who rely on word of mouth**, the largest group in the sample and the one with the strongest stories. Give them the thing they currently get from the friend who follows everything.
2. **People with little or no social media.** For them there is currently nothing except a sign by the road.
3. **Event organizers and venues**, who today advertise only to their existing followers. They are the supply side and the likeliest partners.

The heavy Instagram user with a curated feed is not the first customer. They are served well enough by what they have and they will judge the product against it.

## The business objective

Within the semester, launch a public event-discovery platform covering El Paso and Juárez that gives people advance notice of upcoming events and gives organizers a direct place to publish them. The Project Charter sets the first success criteria: organizer participation, advance notice, and interest in paid featured listings. The Sprint 2 business case tests those assumptions, refines the targets where needed, adds the business-case measures below (returning weekly visitors and events listed per week), and carries the go/no-go recommendation.

## Success measures

| Measure | Why it matters | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organizer participation | Sustainable supply, not team curation forever | At least 20 El Paso and Juárez organizers post events by the end of the pilot run (Project Charter) |
| Advance notice | The problem is information arriving too late to be useful; participants asked for about a week | At least 80% of listed events are shown to users one week before the event (Project Charter) |
| Interest in paid featured listings | Tests whether the charter's monetization idea has real organizer interest | At least 2 event organizers express interest in paying for a featured listing during the pilot run (Project Charter) |
| Returning weekly visitors | The habit the product is trying to create | Set in the Sprint 2 business case |
| Events listed per week | The product is only useful if it is more complete than a feed | Set in the Sprint 2 business case |

## How it could sustain itself

The Project Charter names paid featured or promoted listings as the monetization approach to test. That is a hypothesis, not a proven revenue stream: Sprint 2 evaluates whether organizers show enough interest to support it, and whether the other options the research points at, sponsorship by venues or the city's tourism office and promoted partnerships, should stay as alternatives. Every one of them depends on supply the team has not secured yet, which is why sourcing events is the first strategic risk below.

## What would make the team stop

Kill criteria, so the go/no-go in Sprint 2 has something to test against:

- Events cannot be sourced reliably enough to be more complete than a good Instagram feed.
- Organizers will not submit, and manual curation by the team does not scale past the semester.
- Visitors do not come back the following week.

## Strategic risks

| Risk | Effect | First response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sourcing: the listing is thinner than a curated feed | The product loses to the incumbent it is trying to replace | Start with a small number of categories and venues and be complete in those |
| Discovery: "how would people find out about the standardized way" | Nobody visits the hub | Distribution plan with organizers and the accounts people already follow |
| Scope breadth: two cities, personalization and paid listings | Time goes to secondary features before the core listing is reliable | The date-based listing first; personalization and paid listings in controlled increments, within the charter's scope |
| A free incumbent adds a city-wide listing | The gap closes | Speed, and organizer relationships that a platform does not have |
