## 1. SLI specification

| Field | SLI 1: Event data quality | SLI 2: Website availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition (one precise sentence)** | The share of the 20 event listings sampled each week whose details are real and current when checked against the organizer's source. | The share of the sprint's 336 hours during which the website responds successfully to a check. |
| **Measurement: tool** | Spreadsheet with a checklist that helps us verify the event data against its source. "Good to go" checklist. | A free uptime monitor such as UptimeRobot. Restricting it to the free plans they might offer. |
| **Measurement: schedule** | Every week, on Thursdays. | Automatic checks every 5 mins. |
| **Measurement: owner** | Emmanuel (Data Analysis) | Samuel (Website manager) |
| **SLO (target and time window)** | 95% of sampled listings real and current, each week | 99% of the time up and running, each sprint (two weeks) |
| **Rule check (which of the five rules it follows)** | Avoids absolutes (95%, not 100%); simple to compute (correct listings ÷ 20); perfection can wait (one bad listing per week is tolerated). | Avoids absolutes (99%, not 100%); simple to compute (hours up ÷ 336); perfection can wait (about 3 hours 20 minutes of downtime per sprint is tolerated). |
| **Who would notice** | People using the portal to find events, who would act on a wrong or outdated listing, and the instructor reviewing the content. | Visitors trying to open the site, and the instructor at review time. |

## 2. Measurement plan

| SLI | Method (repeatable by any teammate) | Owner | Schedule | Where results are logged |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Event data quality** | Each week the owner picks 20 listings from the current list using a using a random number generator on the listing numbers, checks each against the organizer's source (existence, date, time, location), and counts how many are real and current. | Emmanuel | Every week, on Thursdays. | Spreadsheet/checklist |
| **Website availability** | An uptime monitor checks the site automatically. At the end of each sprint the owner totals the hours the site responded successfully out of 336. | Samuel | Automatic checks every 5 mins. | Spreadsheet/checklist |

## 3. Five-rule check on our targets

| Rule | SLO 1 (event data) | SLO 2 (availability) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Don't copy today's numbers** | We set 95% so one bad listing a week is allowed while the audit is new; it was not copied from another site or from a current score. | 99% was set from what a student-run site can sustain. |
| **2. Keep it simple** | Correct listings divided by 20. | Hours up divided by 336. |
| **3. Avoid absolutes** | 95%, not 100%. | 99%, not 100%. |
| **4. As few SLOs as possible** | Two SLOs cover what users depend on: right listings and a working site. One number covers both accuracy and freshness. | See SLO 1. |
| **5. Perfection can wait** | One bad listing per week is tolerated. We can tighten the target once the weekly audit is routine. | About 3 hours 20 minutes of downtime per sprint is tolerated. We can tighten later. |

## 4. Share-out tests

| Item | Measurable the same way? (Would any teammate get the same number?) | Realistic, not copied? (What would it cost, and who would notice?) | What we changed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SLI 1 / SLO 1** | Yes, if the sample is chosen by a fixed rule and "real and current" is scored with the written checklist. Without the checklist, teammates could judge "current" differently. | 95% allows one bad listing in 20. Cost: 45-60 minutes each week. Users and the instructor would notice wrong or outdated events. | Added a written "Good to go" checklist so every teammate can keep track of the events the same way. |
| **SLI 2 / SLO 2** | Yes, if an automated monitor does the checking.
