## What this document is

The second of the sprint's two service-level documents: what the team does when a target from the [Service Level Indicators](/sprint-4/service-level-indicators/) is missed. Each objective gets an error budget as a count, a policy for when the budget is spent, an owner and someone to tell; an escalation table sets three severity levels with a time to start fixing and a time to tell the instructor; the incident roles are named for this sprint; and one incident from Sprint 3 gets a blameless review. This is Team 9's file, submitted on 2 November 2026 as the team wrote it. The PDF is the document; this page is its cover.

## What it contains

Five tables, in the document's own words:

1. **Targets.** The two indicators with their target, time window and "who measures": 95 percent of the event details real and current, each week, measured by Emmanuel; the website up and running 99 percent of the time, each sprint, measured by Samuel.
2. **Budgets.** Each objective's budget as a count, "5% of 20 verified event listings = 1 inaccurate listing per week" and "1% of 336 hours ~ 3 hours and 20 minutes of downtime per sprint"; the "policy action" when it is spent, halting event listing, correcting every incorrect listing and doubling the audit sample to 40 for the next week, or freezing feature implementations for the rest of the sprint and writing a note of the issue and the fix; the owner; and "who we tell, and when."
3. **Escalation.** Three severity levels, each with an example on the portal, when fixing starts and when the instructor is told: from the portal down within 48 hours of a deadline (immediately; within one hour) to UI issues, slow pages, typos and misaligned layout (within the current sprint; only if it persists).
4. **Incident roles.** This sprint's incident leader, site fixers, communicator and "everyone else."
5. **Incident review.** A blameless review of a Sprint 3 incident: the team's evidence log and an individual contradiction memo gave the same evidence IDs to different interviews, and the memo carried a cost figure that came from neither the budget nor the estimate; both were caught before the class demo and no business case number changed because of them. With the impact, the timeline from the interviews of 12 to 16 October to the fix on 2 November, and the contributing causes.

## What it feeds

The budgets and the escalation table apply from this sprint on: the weekly audit and the uptime monitor from the Service Level Indicators report against them. The incident review names its causes, evidence IDs assigned in two places and no cross-check between the team log and the individual memos, and the [Sprint 4 change log](/sprint-4/change-log/) carries them as open items.
