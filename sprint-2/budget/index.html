What this document is

The course's Session 11 activity, "Building the Budget", turns an estimate into money in ten steps: the assumptions the cost depends on, the work in packages, effort from more than one method, hours converted to FTE-months, a staffing plan by month, a loaded labor rate, the labor and everything else, the cost of ownership, the risks and the two reserves, and the budget time-phased into a request. The team's budget starts from the Estimation Appendix: the range of 394 to 852 hours for the event-goer slice, and the planning value of 603.67 hours.

This page carries the two documents the team delivered, as the team wrote them. First the one-page budget summary of 6 October 2026, the version a sponsor reads. Then the ten-step budget of 4 October 2026 that supports it, step by step. The files are the Team's PDFs above. The charts are redrawn from the team's figures in the portal's palette, so they read in both themes and in print, and the revision history at the foot of the page records where the page and the delivered files differ.

Schedule gap

The charter's top-down number is 8 weeks at 50 team-hours: 400 h, about $32,870 of labor. The bottom-up slice prices at 603.67 h and $49,607, which is 203.67 h or about 51 percent more, and takes roughly 12.1 weeks at the same pace. Closing the gap means a smaller slice or a longer timeline.

Notes to rows 3 and 5

The 65/17/10/8 percentages are shares of the delivery hours (545.17), not of the full 603.67. The 0.15 is an FTE figure, not a percentage of hours. It means 15% of one full-time person each month, which is 0.15 × 130 = 19.5 hours a month, or 58.5 hours over 3 months. This is how the hours are carved out:

  1. Start with the total: 603.67 hours.
  2. Take the PM's share off first: 603.67 − 58.5 = 545.17 hours left for delivery.
  3. Split those 545.17 hours 65/17/10/8 among the delivery roles.

The Session 9 estimate priced hours at $61.63, a loaded rate per paid hour; this budget prices them per productive hour, $82.18.

Step 3: effort more than one way

Three-point (PERT)

E = (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6. O, the optimistic case, is 394 hours, if everything goes well. M, the most likely, is 594 hours. P, the pessimistic case, is 852 hours, if real problems hit. E = 3,622 ÷ 6 = 603.67 hours.

Why E is above M: the bad case is farther from most likely than the good case is, so it pulls the answer up. Spread = (P − O) ÷ 6 = (852 − 394) ÷ 6 = 76.33 hours: a typical miss. Step 9 reuses it.

Do the methods land within 25 percent of each other? The rule of thumb: if they differ by more than 25 percent, find out why before pricing anything. The team's answer: not needed, the spread is 23 percent. The planning value of 603.67 hours sits 22.9 percent above the 491 hour analogous midpoint, inside the 25 percent rule. Bottom-up and PERT are identical because the package hours were distributed from the PERT expected value, so analogous is the only independent comparison in the table.

Five sanity checks

  1. Totals match. Add the staffing plan by month and compare it to Step 4. Ours: 4.64 FTE-months by month (1.667 + 1.667 + 1.310) = 4.64 FTE-months in Step 4.
  2. Labor share is plausible. Divide labor by the base build. Ours: $49,607 ÷ $49,622 = 99.97 percent. Nearly all cost is people because every tool and service stays on a free tier.
  3. Estimates agree. Compare the effort estimates. Ours: 491 to 603.67 hours, within 23 percent.
  4. Reserves trace to risks. Every contingency dollar links to a named risk in the Step 9 table. Ours: $3,061 + $2,663 + $719 = $6,443, all three named in the Step 9 table.
  5. Top-down comparison. Put the sponsor's number next to yours and explain the gap. Sponsor's number: 400 hours (8 weeks × 50 team-hours), about $32,870 of labor. Ours: 603.67 hours, $49,607 of labor. The gap is 203.67 hours, or $16,737, about 51 percent more. Eight weeks at our 50 team-hours a week only fits 400 hours, but the seven stories in the slice price at 603.67 hours, which takes about 12.1 weeks at that pace. To close the gap either the slice gets smaller or the timeline gets longer.

Check 5 in action: the sponsor's number

What did the sponsor ask for, in time or money? 8 weeks at 50 team-hours a week, so 400 hours (about $32,870). Our budget says the work is 4.64 FTE-months.

4.64 FTE-months ÷ 2.8 months = 1.66 people. 4.64 FTE-months ÷ 1.85 months (the sponsor's time) = 2.51 people. Every pair of people is a path where a misunderstanding can happen. 5 people means 10 of them.

What is still open

  • The rates behind every figure are assumed, not measured: hours per function point, hours per person-month for the comparable, the match and the uplift, the burden factor, and 130 productive hours per FTE-month. The team replaces them with its own numbers after three sprints, and the budget moves with them.
  • The sponsor's number and the team's differ by 203.67 hours, about $16,737. Either the slice gets smaller or the timeline gets longer; that decision is the team's, to be recorded in the change log when it is made.
  • The sponsor confirmed on 5 October 2026 that a Business Case and an ROI Analysis are not Sprint 2 deliverables; when the course calls for them, this budget is their cost side. The change log records every revision to this page and what it affected.
